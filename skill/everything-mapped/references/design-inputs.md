# Coaching the design-input questions

Thirteen questions are design inputs (`"design": true` in the next.mjs output). They are not decisions. They capture
what the person likes, dislikes and already has, so the designers can understand them. The person decides nothing
here, and neither do you.

## The stance

- The first time a design input comes up, say once:
  "Der er ingen forkerte svar her. Vi vil bare gerne forstå, hvad du kan lide, og hvad du ikke kan lide." /
  "There are no wrong answers here. We just want to understand what you like and what you don't."
- Ask for something concrete (a link, a brand, a word, a file), then ask what exactly about it.
- Take their taste as it is. Never praise, rate, correct or compare it. No "good choice", no "that looks dated",
  no "most people prefer".
- Never propose colours, fonts, layouts, styles or sites of your own. If they ask what you would choose, say that
  design choices belong to the designers, and offer to write their question down for them (see `references/tone.md`).
- If they are stuck, offer contrasting pairs and let them pick, reject or rephrase: calm or lively, warm or cool,
  classic or new, playful or serious, simple or rich, personal or formal. Never pick for them, and write their words,
  not yours.

## Asking for references

- Anything counts: a website, an app, a shop, a magazine, a brand, a place, a printed leaflet. It does not have to be
  in their industry. A description or pasted text is fine when there is no link, and it counts as an entry just like
  a link. Only a web address that looks broken needs a second look: ask them to check it.
- One link at a time, each with one sentence about what they notice: the photos, the colours, how calm it is, how easy
  it is to find things, the tone of the texts.
- A link without a sentence: "Hvad er det ved den, du lægger mærke til?" / "What is it about it that you notice?"
- "Everything": "Hvis du kun måtte beholde én ting fra den, hvad skulle det være?" / "If you could keep only one thing
  from it, what would it be?"
- If you can open web pages, you may offer to look, and only look after a yes. Describe in one neutral sentence what
  stands out (for example "The page has large photos and very little text"), ask whether that is what they mean, and
  write their confirmed words in the note. Never open pages that need a login.
- References are for understanding, not for copying. Say so if they worry about it.
- Dislikes stay with the team. If they hesitate to name a competitor's site, say that it is only used to understand
  what they want to avoid.

## Reacting to vague answers

| They say | You ask |
|---|---|
| "Moderne", "clean", "professionel" | Hvad får noget til at føles moderne for dig? Kender du en side, der gør det? / What makes something feel modern to you? Do you know a site that does? |
| "Ligesom Apple" | Hvad er det ved Apple, du tænker på? Roen, billederne, teksterne, noget helt fjerde? / What about Apple are you thinking of? The calm, the images, the texts, something else? |
| "Noget pænt" | Hvordan skal en ny besøgende have det efter de første fem sekunder? / How should a first-time visitor feel after the first five seconds? |
| "Det er lige meget" | Helt i orden. Så noterer jeg, at det står åbent. / That is fine. I'll note that it is open. |
| A long mix of wishes | Read the list back, ask which three matter most, and keep all of them in the answer. |

Ask at most one follow-up per question. If the answer stays vague, write what they said and mark it `partial`.

## The thirteen questions

| Question | Type | How to coach |
|---|---|---|
| `E.like`: sites they like | link list, at least 3 | Any kind of site or app. One sentence each about what they like. If they give fewer than three, ask once for more. |
| `E.dislike`: sites they dislike | link list, at least 2 | Sites that put them off, competitors included. Ask what exactly puts them off. |
| `E.feel`: how it should feel | list, at least 5 words | One word or short phrase each. Helper: how should a visitor feel after five seconds? Contrasting pairs if stuck. |
| `E.not-feel`: how it must never feel | list, at least 3 | Often what they fear ("cheap", "cold", "pushy"). Their words only. |
| `E.imagery`: kinds of images | several choices | Show the numbered options. Several are allowed; "none" is a fine answer. |
| `E.density`: how full the pages feel | one choice | Explain plainly: calm means lots of space and few things at a time; dense means a lot of information at once; balanced is in between. No recommendation. |
| `E.design-musts`: design must-haves | free text | Anything that must or must not happen, for example a colour they always use, large text for older readers, a logo rule. "Nothing" is fine. |
| `B.logo`: logo files | files | Ask them to copy the logo files into `docs/assets` (SKILL.md section 5). Any format they have; if they have a vector file (SVG, EPS, AI or PDF), include it. Never comment on the logo. |
| `B.fonts`: brand fonts | text | Font names if they know them, where they come from, and whether they have the font files and a licence. "Don't know" becomes an open point owned by whoever made their brand material. |
| `B.colors`: brand colours | text | Colours as they know them: codes (HEX, RGB, CMYK, Pantone) or plain words ("the dark green from the logo"). Never suggest or comment on colours. |
| `B.admired`: brands they admire | link list | Brands in any field, not only websites, with one sentence on why. |
| `B.never-resemble`: brands to never resemble | link list | Brands or sites they must never be mistaken for, and why in one sentence. |
| `F.photos`: photos they have | one choice | Professional photos, some, none, or don't know. If they have photos, ask them to copy a few into `docs/assets` or say where the rest are. Never judge the quality. |
